Most independent trades businesses lose more admin time to answering calls, chasing quotes, and juggling bookings than to paperwork itself. Automation realistically removes the repetitive, time-sensitive parts of that — answering calls, sending reminders, chasing cold quotes — while decisions that need judgement still need you.

## Where the time actually goes

Ask most electricians, plumbers, or builders where their week disappears to, and it's rarely the technical work. It's the surrounding admin: the call that comes in while you're up a ladder, the quote you meant to follow up on three days ago, the customer who calls to ask "is it still on for Tuesday?" because nobody sent a reminder.

None of that is complicated work. It's just work that has to happen at the exact moment you're doing something else, which means it either gets dropped or it gets done badly late at night.

## What's realistic to automate

Three categories of admin genuinely lend themselves to automation for a trades business:

- **Answering and capturing enquiries.** A missed call doesn't have to mean a missed job. An AI system can answer, capture what's needed, and either book straightforward jobs directly or flag anything that needs your judgement.
- **Chasing and reminding.** Quotes that go cold because nobody followed up, and jobs that get missed because nobody sent a reminder, are both solvable with automated, well-timed messages rather than a mental to-do list.
- **Logging what happened.** Call notes, job details, and enquiry records that would otherwise live in your head or a notebook can be captured automatically as the interaction happens.

## What still needs you

Pricing a non-standard job, making a judgement call on a tricky access issue, or handling an upset customer — none of that should be automated away, and a well-built system won't try. The honest answer to "how much time could this save me" is: it depends how much of your week is spent on the first three categories versus genuine judgement calls. For most trades businesses we've scoped, it's a meaningful share — often enough to matter, rarely enough to replace the parts of the job that actually need you.

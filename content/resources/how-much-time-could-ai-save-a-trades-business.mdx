Most independent trades businesses lose more admin time to answering calls, chasing quotes, and juggling bookings than to paperwork itself. Automation realistically removes the repetitive, time-sensitive parts of that — answering calls, sending reminders, chasing cold quotes — while decisions that need judgement still need you.

## Where the time actually goes

Ask most electricians, plumbers, or builders where their week disappears to, and it's rarely the technical work. It's the surrounding admin: the call that comes in while you're up a ladder, the quote you meant to follow up on three days ago, the customer who calls to ask "is it still on for Tuesday?" because nobody sent a reminder.

None of that is complicated work. It's just work that has to happen at the exact moment you're doing something else, which means it either gets dropped or it gets done badly late at night, after a full day on the tools, when the motivation to write up notes or chase a quote is at its lowest.

There's also a second, quieter cost: the enquiries that never even register as lost time, because they simply went to someone else before you got the chance to answer. That's harder to feel day-to-day than a pile of unanswered messages, but it's often the bigger number.

## What's realistic to automate

Three categories of admin genuinely lend themselves to automation for a trades business:

- **Answering and capturing enquiries.** A missed call doesn't have to mean a missed job. An AI system can answer, capture what's needed, and either book straightforward jobs directly or flag anything that needs your judgement. See our [enquiries and leads](/services/enquiries-and-leads) service for how this works in practice.
- **Chasing and reminding.** Quotes that go cold because nobody followed up, and jobs that get missed because nobody sent a reminder, are both solvable with automated, well-timed messages rather than a mental to-do list — covered under [follow-ups and nurture](/services/follow-ups-and-nurture).
- **Logging what happened.** Call notes, job details, and enquiry records that would otherwise live in your head or a notebook can be captured automatically as the interaction happens, which is the basis of [admin and back-office](/services/admin-and-back-office).

## What still needs you

Pricing a non-standard job, making a judgement call on a tricky access issue, or handling an upset customer — none of that should be automated away, and a well-built system won't try. This is the line we draw deliberately: a system that tries to sound confident about things it genuinely can't judge ends up costing you more trust than the time it saves.

The honest answer to "how much time could this save me" is: it depends how much of your week is spent on the first three categories versus genuine judgement calls. For most trades businesses we've scoped, it's a meaningful share — often enough to matter, rarely enough to replace the parts of the job that actually need you.

## A rough way to think about it yourself

Before assuming a number, it's worth actually counting for a week. Keep a simple tally of:

- How many calls or messages you didn't answer in real time, and roughly how long it took to get back to each one
- How many quotes you sent that you never explicitly followed up on
- How many bookings needed a reminder text or call that you sent manually — or didn't send at all

Most business owners are surprised by the second and third numbers more than the first. Missed calls are visible and annoying, so they tend to get noticed and occasionally chased. Quotes that quietly go cold are less visible — nothing rings, nothing sits in a notification badge — which is exactly why they're the easiest category to underestimate.

It's also worth tracking a fourth thing over that same week: how many evenings you spent catching up on admin after a full day of physical work. That's often the number that makes the case most clearly, because it's the one that actually affects you directly rather than showing up as an abstract missed opportunity — a genuinely free evening is worth more to most tradespeople than an abstract percentage on a spreadsheet.

## Why this varies so much between businesses

A one-person operation doing mostly emergency call-outs has a very different time profile to a two-van business doing planned installs booked weeks in advance. The first loses more to missed calls during jobs; the second loses more to quotes going cold over a longer decision window. Neither is more or less "suited" to automation — the shape of what gets automated is just different, which is why we scope this individually rather than quoting a blanket percentage that would be more marketing than fact.

When an AI receptionist can't confidently handle something, it escalates to a real person rather than guessing — that's a rule we build into every system, not an afterthought. The business owner sets where that line sits, and can move it at any time.

## The real question isn't "will it get things wrong"

Every system — human or AI — occasionally mishandles something. A new receptionist misquotes a price on their first week too. The real question business owners should be asking is: what happens next, and who's in control of where the boundaries are?

## How we design for it

Every system we build starts with an explicit list of what it's allowed to decide on its own, and what it must hand off. Booking a routine appointment within normal hours: fine to handle directly. An unusual request, a complaint, or anything involving a price that isn't standard: escalate, every time.

That escalation isn't a vague "it'll figure it out" promise — it's a specific set of rules we agree with you during scoping, and you can change them at any point once the system is live. If something happens that you didn't anticipate, we adjust the rules, not just apologise for it.

## Your data, your control

Business owners considering AI are usually more worried about losing control than excited about the technology, and that's a reasonable instinct. Practically, that means:

- You know what data is captured and where it's stored — see our [privacy approach](/legal/privacy) for the specifics.
- You can review call summaries and enquiry logs, not just trust that they happened correctly.
- Escalation rules are yours to set and change, not fixed by us and left alone.

An AI system that can't be adjusted once it's live isn't one we'd want to build for you either. If you're weighing this up for your own business, a [discovery call](/contact) is the fastest way to see exactly how the control and escalation would work for your specific setup.
